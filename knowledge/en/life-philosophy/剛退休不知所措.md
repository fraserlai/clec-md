> **In 30 seconds**: If retirement leaves you with lots of time and no idea how to use it, **don't blame yourself** — we spent many years learning to work, and we can give ourselves time to learn to be retired. James doesn't suggest hunting for a grand new purpose; instead, **try it for two weeks and start with one small thing you can do**: build a small daily rhythm, grow a plant, find a café, pick one hobby to explore slowly, use AI to record your life experience, document and share your life, and after two weeks keep what makes you want to continue.[^1]

## Why you feel lost

"At work, every day had a schedule, tasks, people waiting on you. After decades of being busy you finally retire, suddenly have huge amounts of time, don't know how to arrange it — and may even feel life has lost its center." James's view: it isn't a flaw in you; you **just haven't learned it yet**. You don't need to find a new life purpose right away.[^1]

## Seven steps (try them for two weeks)

Summarized from post 0049; several are James's own pleasures:[^1]

1. **Give each day a little rhythm** — do three small things daily: walk outside for twenty or thirty minutes in the morning; spend twenty minutes during the day learning something that interests you; in the evening write three sentences (what I did today, what I noticed, what I want to try tomorrow). Fill the rest with chores, family and rest — no need to pack the day; finishing just one is fine, with no catching up.
2. **Grow a plant to give each day something to look forward to** — James loves growing flowers and fruit; watching seeds sprout, flower and fruit, "you feel the power of life." Visit a flower market or a market plant stall, tell the seller how much sun you get and whether it's for a balcony or indoors, and ask for easy varieties; chili or tomato seeds from your kitchen work for experiments too. Photograph day one — that's your first life record.
3. **Find a café and practice savoring time** — pick a day each week to sit for an hour or two with a book and a few notes; when the barista isn't busy, ask why they love coffee — "you'll see someone's passion for their work," and talking with young people may bring new ideas. Don't drink coffee? A teahouse, library or park works too.
4. **Pick one hobby you're willing to explore slowly** — James keeps fish: observing their habits, aquarium layout, how different fish breed. It could also be photography, cooking, painting, singing, history or culture. **No need to cultivate five hobbies at once — pick one and try it.**
5. **Learn to use AI to organize your life experience** — stay curious about the world after retiring. If you don't know where to begin, tell an AI: "I want to record my life story. Ask me one question at a time, starting with my first job. After I answer, turn it into a ~300-word life note in my own voice, without adding anything I didn't say." Don't like typing? Talk into your phone for three minutes and have AI tidy it up; **read it back yourself to make sure it's accurate**. Over time it may become a book of your own.
6. **Record your life and dare to share it** — new leaves, the aquarium, a coffee you loved, a travel view, and your investing and retirement story (how you started, what you feared, what mistakes you made, what you learned). Post on X or Facebook, or make a video — no special equipment needed; **get consent before publishing other people's photos or words**. "Don't worry about how many people watch; just start… its value doesn't need anyone's likes to prove it."
7. **After two weeks, keep what makes you want to continue** — ask yourself: "Which thing makes me want to do it again tomorrow?" "When did I feel today went well?" Give more time to what you like and swap out what doesn't fit; each week, arrange one meet-up with friends or a walk with family to keep contact with others. **No need to turn hobbies into work or produce results every day; resting with peace of mind is part of retired life too.**

> "Tomorrow, why not step outside, bring a plant home, take a photo, and write: 'Today I started practicing my retired life.'"[^1]
