> **In 30 seconds**: **Buy at market immediately, all at once — then don't sell.** No splitting, no waiting for a pullback — "you're not a god; waiting is the dumbest strategy." The *why* (a lump sum's expected value, each win-rate figure's period and method, the one case where splitting makes sense, how it differs from regular monthly investing) is on [[單筆還是分批|Lump Sum or Split It Up?]]; this page only collects how people actually ask and how James answered each time. The only reason to pause: **your allocation percentages aren't settled** — settle them first (see [[現金是空氣|Cash Is Air]]), then buy in one go.

## Situational variants (how people actually ask it)

- "I just sold my house / cancelled my insurance — invest it all at once or split it?"
- "The market's about to hit a new high — shouldn't I wait for a pullback?"
- "Can I feed it in over a few months? Going all in at once is terrifying."
- "I took out a personal loan — should the borrowed money go in all at once?"
- "I'm very worried about losses — all in, split, or 433?"
- "The market is falling right now — shouldn't I buy a bit later?"
- "This is my parents' money and I can't accept losing it — can I put it in fixed deposits and invest only part?"

## Answers over time

- **2026-11-21 (00585)**: with the market falling that week, someone again asked whether to buy later → "**choosing when to buy means you're a short-term trader**… isn't it falling now? Afraid to buy when it falls, afraid to buy when it rises — then you'll never buy"; people who worry about declines actually aren't suited to investing. The deck repeats "stop asking whether to split… waiting is the dumbest strategy" and "a lump sum has a 70% higher win rate than timing." In the same session a 24-year-old selling a home her parents paid for, unable to accept a loss, wanted 70% in fixed deposits → deposits are a guaranteed loss; move the money to Hong Kong and **put part into HK-listed Nasdaq-100 funds first, stop if it falls, add once it's in profit** — for someone who can't stomach losing principal, he allows a staged entry.[^11]
- **2026-11-14 (00584)**: he opened with "buy at market as soon as you have money… no need to wait"; but he also said "it looks like the standard answer, but **not everyone can swallow this medicine**" — many sell their house, cancel insurance, go all in and start doubting everything after three months of decline; weigh your own temperament. To those who bought in the 700s: buyers in the 300s in 2022 who watched it fall to 250 felt the same; in five years people will envy you for buying at 750 — **buy first, win first**.[^12]
- **2026-08-29 (00580)**: opening the session — "Buy at market as soon as you have money and your win rate is 70%; if you split, hesitate, or wait for the market to fall, you're playing a game you can't win."[^1]
- **2026-08-15 (00578)**: "Buy at market as soon as you have money — no splitting." With AI developing beyond imagination, "the sooner you get the money in, the better."[^2]
- **2026-07-23 (post 0039)**: retells the 2022 house-sale case — he suggested splitting then only because the person wasn't ready; splitting "only feels" less scary. Statistically the lump sum wins (about 97% for 2009–2026, about 87% counting from the 2000 peak): "99% of people treat how they buy as far too important." (How the figures are computed: [[單筆還是分批|Lump Sum or Split It Up?]].)[^3]
- **2026-07-18 (00574)**: "I sold my house — should I buy in immediately or split it?" — "The red text in the video is this big; if you still don't get it, I don't know what to tell you." Buy immediately.[^4]
- **2026-07-04 (00572)**: the weekly deck's fixed page starts carrying the line: "Stop asking 'is it OK to buy like this? Should I split it?' And don't talk about waiting for a dip."[^5]
- **2026-06 (00571)**: a self-described novice, very worried about losses, asked: all in, split, or 433? James: "**You should leave the market.**" — worry about losses isn't a buying-method problem; it's a question of whether you should invest at all.[^6]
- **2026-05 (00565)**: why he no longer gives two answers: "'After cancelling my insurance, lump sum or split?' — stop asking that; buy at market as soon as you have money." In teaching, give two answers and everyone asks which one to use now — "one begets two, two beget three."[^7]
- **2024-10 (00489)**: with the market near a new high, someone had invested only 1% and wanted to spread it over months — "buy in right now… once it hits a new high you'll be even more afraid to buy." He was candid about the goal: **"I just want to kick everyone into the market — whatever it takes, as long as you buy in."**[^8]
- **2024-01 (00455)**: "Can I buy, or should I wait for a drop?" — "If you have money, buy. **How you buy doesn't matter**… so what if it drops? You're not selling. **Not selling is the mark of a master.**"[^9]
- **2022-11 (00382, a bear-market year)**: the looser version then: "Buy when you have money; you can split it and buy slowly, buy more when it falls more, **but never think the market will keep falling and wait**"; once bought, don't sell.[^10]

> 🔎 **How the answer evolved**: in the 2022 bear market James allowed splitting (but not waiting); in 2026-11 he also said outright that the standard answer isn't for everyone, still allowing a staged entry for someone who "can't lose her parents' money"; in 2023, chapter 44 of *Origins and Today* argued statistically that a lump sum wins; from 2026 the weekly deck simply says "stop asking whether to split." From "you may split" to "stop asking," it's the same road as [[從擇時操作到打死不賣|From Market Timing to Never Selling]] — **what decides success isn't how you buy but that you never sell afterward**. Full evolution: [[單筆還是分批|Lump Sum or Split It Up?]].

> ⚠️ An educational summary, not personalized investment advice. Before buying in one go, settle your allocation and [[十五年現金流|15 years of cash flow]] — only once you can survive the worst case does "how to buy" even come up.
