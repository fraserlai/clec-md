> **In 30 seconds**: For US workers with spare cash, the contribution order is **① fill the Roth first → ② a regular brokerage account → never put money into a Pre-Tax (Traditional) IRA/401K**. James flatly calls Pre-Tax accounts an "**asset poison pill**," with three fatal problems: **RMDs force withdrawals** into your top marginal bracket, a later **Roth conversion is still taxed high**, and worst of all it's **double-taxed as an inheritance (estate tax + the heir's income tax)**. Which account holds which asset is covered in [[帳戶類型該放什麼資產|what goes in Roth vs Traditional]].[^1]

## Contribution priority

With spare cash:[^1]

1. **Fill the Roth first** (Roth growth is tax-free for life, and so are withdrawals).
2. **Then a regular (taxable) brokerage account.**
3. **Do not** put money into a **Pre-Tax (Traditional) IRA/401K**.

(Related: which *asset* goes in which account — aggressive QLD/QQQ in Roth, conservative BOXX/[[QQQI]]/cash in Traditional or taxable — is in [[帳戶類型該放什麼資產|what goes in Roth vs Traditional]]. This page is about whether to use the account *type* at all.)

**Don't contribute to an HSA (Health Savings Account) either.** The 00578 deck puts it most bluntly: "I don't recommend US residents contribute to an HSA or pre-tax retirement accounts (IRA/401K) — only ever contribute to Roth… **HSAs and pre-tax retirement accounts are scam accounts: you save tax up front, and afterward it's a money prison!**" His reasoning: you should decide how your own money is used, not the government or the banks; an HSA dictates that the money be spent on medical costs, "but our money is never sold — being forced to spend it makes it worthless." (The same logic applies to education accounts like 529/RESP; see [[要不要讓小孩貸款上大學|Should my child take out loans for college?]].)[^5]

## Pre-Tax's three fatal problems

### 1. RMDs force withdrawals → pushed into your top bracket

A Pre-Tax account has **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** at a statutory age. Once the balance has grown large (e.g. into the tens of millions), those forced withdrawals push your **marginal rate into the top bracket** — you can't choose "not to take it this year."[^1]

### 2. A retirement-age Roth conversion is still taxed high

"Can't I just convert it to Roth slowly after I retire?" — the rate is just as high. Example: $2M in a Pre-Tax IRA at ~15%/yr — the growth alone means you'd need to **convert ~$340k a year** to finish in ten years, and every conversion is added to that year's income, so **the rate won't be low**.[^1]

### 3. As an inheritance: double taxation (the worst)

Pre-Tax IRA's biggest problem isn't RMDs — it's **being double-taxed when it becomes an estate**:[^1]

- **Total estate below the exemption (~$13M)**: no estate tax, but **when heirs withdraw from the Pre-Tax IRA they owe ordinary income tax**. Example: inherit a $2M Pre-Tax IRA that forces ~$340k/yr out; the heir already has income, so stacked on top the **marginal rate can hit the top bracket**. The tax you didn't pay, your heirs pay — more.
- **Pre-Tax IRA above the exemption (e.g. $20M)**: it's **taxed once by estate tax**, and then when heirs withdraw the remainder (**principal + growth, all of it**) it's **taxed again as income** — **estate tax once, income tax again: double taxation**.

> By contrast, **Roth** has no RMD, tax-free growth and withdrawals, and passes cleanly to heirs. This also echoes [[別為錢賣命|don't sell your life for money]]: the surplus you can't spend becomes inheritance — and Pre-Tax makes that inheritance taxed twice at the handoff.

## Already stuck with a Pre-Tax IRA — how do you get it out? (conversion tools and timing)

If you already hold a Pre-Tax (traditional) IRA, James's advice isn't to leave it alone but to **convert it to Roth gradually in your low-income years, using a "conversion calculator" to size each year's conversion**. 00476 is dedicated to this:[^3]

- **Use a conversion calculator**: a CLEC student (Linda) built a **bilingual online calculator** for Traditional→Roth, posted in the channel's materials. You enter a few numbers — **pre-tax IRA balance, expected annual return, your age, your income** — and it outputs **how much to convert each year** (Amount to Convert), so you move money out without being pushed into a high bracket.
- **Convert in "low-income years"**: the key is to catch the years **after retiring but before Social Security / before RMDs kick in** — income is lowest then, so converting Pre-Tax to Roth in tranches carries the lightest tax. Once converted, the money's growth and withdrawals in the Roth are **tax-free for life**, with no RMD and clean for heirs (see above).
- **Beware the insurance pitch**: James specifically warns that **insurance agents increasingly try to roll your Traditional IRA into their insurance accounts** (even invoking things like a 7702 policy as a "tax tool"). That usually swaps a tax problem for a pricier, more locked-in insurance problem — don't fall for it (see [[儲蓄險與壽險陷阱]]).[^3]

> ⚠️ **No deck backing**: 00476 has no corresponding deck; this section is transcript-only, and the calculator's demo figures (age, amounts) vary with your inputs and shouldn't be treated as fixed — rely on your own calculation and a tax professional.


**2026 addendum: when to convert (00584 / 00585)** — James's own practice is to **convert a fixed amount every year on the first trading day of January** (January 2nd or 3rd), on the assumption that year-end prices are usually higher than at the start; he doesn't try to "time" the swings in between. When a member asked whether to convert less because the market was falling → "not earlier, not later — convert per plan on the first trading day each year." But if **you haven't converted yet this year**, a dip is good timing — the same dollar amount moves more shares.[^6] In the same session he described the US "three pools": **a taxable brokerage account for PAL pledging**, **the Roth as the PAL account's reservoir** (if the brokerage account falls too far and borrowing capacity drops, move assets over from the Roth), and **the Traditional IRA converted to Roth each year**.[^6]

## A new variant: the Trump Account (a disguised Traditional IRA for kids)

The **Trump Account**, launched July 4, 2026, is an account opened early for children born in the US or with legal status. In essence it's **a disguised Traditional IRA** — so this page's conclusion applies: **not recommended**. The practical call:[^4]

- The government puts **US$1,000** in when the account is opened: that's free money — take it. CLEC friends can open one for their child and collect the 1,000.
- **But don't add your own money.** Like a Traditional 401K it has "poison" properties; it isn't worth locking your own money into.
- For now the only fund approved is **SPYM** (the low-cost S&P 500 version from SPY's fund company — deliberately **not QQQ/QQQM**), with an annual contribution cap of about **US$5,000**. So: **use the government's 1,000 to buy SPYM and invest nothing further.**

(Another example of the government steering you toward the S&P 500 rather than the Nasdaq in the name of "protecting retail investors," echoing [[價值一億元的投資人生講座|the first-lesson lecture]]'s observations on system design.)

## Non-US persons should avoid US accounts entirely

James also stresses that **non-US tax residents should not touch US accounts** — beyond the retirement-account tax issues, there's estate tax ($60k exemption, 40% rate) and inheritance difficulty. Non-US investors should use Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETFs instead — see [[UCITS-ETF-非美國投資人|UCITS ETFs for non-US investors]].[^2]
