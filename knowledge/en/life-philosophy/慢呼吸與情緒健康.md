> **In 30 seconds**: James's own admission — **every time he's on Clubhouse answering messages, his blood sugar spikes; that's the stress hormone at work.** Most people think their job isn't stressful, they just don't notice they're "holding their breath." His fix is simple: **breathe in slowly for 5.5 seconds, hold briefly, breathe out slowly for 5.5 seconds** — you don't need to do it around the clock, just find ten minutes a day, maybe with some soft music, and do a few rounds of this breathing. Lower stress hormones mean less accumulated toxicity in the body. He also flags one shot most 40-somethings skip: the shingles vaccine, which he says can cut dementia risk by roughly **25%**, for under NT$20,000 total.[^1][^2]

## Mood affects health more than people realize

James notes that **mood has an outsized effect on health**, and most people don't realize they're under stress because "you don't feel stressed at work simply because you're holding your breath." His own example: every time he answers messages or comments on Clubhouse, or goes live, he knows his blood sugar rises (he mentions around 120) — that's the **stress hormone** at work, even when his tone of voice sounds calm.[^1]

## The technique: 5.5 seconds in, 5.5 seconds out

James's method is simple and actionable, and the point is to go **slow**:[^1]

- **Inhale for 5.5 seconds** — if you finish inhaling before 5.5 seconds is up, hold briefly.
- **Then exhale slowly for 5.5 seconds.**
- **You don't need to do this all day** — pick a break in your day, or put on some soft music, and do about ten minutes of this breathing to reset your mood.
- **You can use it in the moment too** — while talking or in a meeting, practice slow inhales and exhales instead of getting worked up; "that's probably a bit better for your body."

His own takeaway: **breathing slowly at work improves mood, lowers stress, lowers the stress hormone, and reduces the toxins your body accumulates.** He also acknowledges blood sugar is tied to mood, stress hormones, and food — even eating the same food, it's sometimes hard to control, and emotion is the variable.[^1]

> 🔗 This is the same spirit as [[快樂是人權|Happiness Is a Human Right]] — not chasing a zero-stress life, but **actively lowering the cost your body pays wherever you can control it.**

## An overlooked shot: the shingles vaccine and dementia risk

Toward the end of the same session, James flagged a health investment he thinks most people overlook: **consider a self-paid shingles vaccine if you're over 40** — not primarily to prevent shingles itself, but to **lower dementia risk**.[^2]

- **The mechanism**: carrying the shingles virus can keep irritating the nervous system; chronic inflammation of the brain's nerve pathways is one contributor to dementia in some elderly patients.
- **The study he cites**: a medical journal reportedly found that vaccination can **reduce elderly dementia risk by roughly 25%**.
- **Cost and process**: the vaccine requires two doses about two months apart; each dose, including doctor's fee and outpatient fee, runs roughly NT$8,000–9,000 — **under NT$20,000 total** for both. Immunology, infectious disease, or family medicine departments at teaching hospitals can administer it; just ask to pay out of pocket.
- James's takeaway: for "under NT$20,000," anyone 40 or older should consider it, to avoid the ongoing risk of chronic irritation and inflammation from the shingles virus.
