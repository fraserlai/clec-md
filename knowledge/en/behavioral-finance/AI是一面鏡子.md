> **In 30 seconds**: A member offered an observation in the chat room — **AI is like a mirror**: ask a fine-grained question, get a fine-grained answer; ask a crude one, get a crude one. So "AI didn't raise our understanding; it answers on top of the understanding we already had." James said "your discovery is exactly right," and added a second danger: **AI tends to agree with you.** Put together: **AI will not grow awareness for you.** Its one valuable use is to **falsify yourself** — ask about the things you don't believe, and make it explain why you're wrong.[^1]

## The member's observation: a mirror, not a teacher

In the Google era everyone found different material and understood it differently. The AI era looks more level, but isn't — **the depth of your question is the depth of your understanding**, and the answer will not exceed it. Her follow-up was sharper: if everyone's birth environment and educational resources differ, and AI only reflects that layer, **are we permanently sealed off from voices outside our comfort zone?**[^1]

## James's explanation: it's an agent, and your entry point decides the answer

- **AI is an agent** — your secretary, your consultant. "The CEO calls in a consultant and says 'this, this and this,' and the consultant says 'yes, I'll do it' — **they do it the boss's way**."[^1]
- **Mechanically, it's a probability distribution**: "Your entry point goes in, and the probability distribution spreads out from that point in the neural network to give you an answer. Enter from a different point and you get a different answer."[^1]
- Therefore: "**AI will not exceed humans** — not so far. However high your knowledge goes, AI only reaches that far." Its real advantage is **faster, more complete search**.[^1]

## The second danger: it agrees with you (no loyal ministers, only a blind king)

The member went on: she found AI tended to agree with her — and when she corrected it, it agreed that her correction was right too. "So I don't think AI can help me find the holes in my own thinking."[^1]

James's analogy came from his corporate career:[^1]

> "In your first three years at a company you get advice and help. Once you become a manager, nobody gives you advice — and nobody forgives your mistakes. **Once you're the CEO, you don't hear the truth.**"
>
> "So as managers we were careful: is what my subordinate says flattery, or genuine advice? **There are no treacherous ministers and no loyal ones — only a blind king.**"

**AI itself is neutral.** What turns it into a flattering court is the user.

## The right use: falsify yourself with it

James's method is concrete — **don't ask it about things you believe** (you already know them; you'll only get agreement):[^1]

> "You should ask it about **things you yourself don't quite believe**… Ask it: '**Is QQQ really the best in the world? Is there something better than QQQ? I think QQQ is wrong**' — and see how it answers. **Put the wrong things forward, not the right ones**, and watch what it says."

Another member named the practice: "**talk to AI from a zero-trust posture**." This is the operational version of [[世界不是真的|"spend the effort falsifying yourself"]]: that page says ChatGPT's investing output is a "textbook-fed academic scribe"; this page adds — **even a scribe can be aimed at attacking your own system, if you ask that way**.[^1]

## Awareness is the only way past your environment

To the worry that environment caps understanding, James's answer is **awareness**:[^1]

- "Your father was a civil servant and nobody around you invested successfully — how do you break the limits your environment set? **You must become aware** — like Socrates, discovering what others don't know."
- **Doubt everything**: "All the books and theories in the world — only through doubt can you become aware. So **don't take anything handed to you as proof**. AI gives you an answer? Don't believe it either. Doubt it."
- Otherwise you are just "**a parrot** that knows many languages and many facts." His estimate, characteristically harsh: "**99.99% of people in society are parrots** — ask them, 'is this something you heard?' Of course it is, it all came from the news."

> 🔗 In the same session: "**What human value is left? The architect** — only people with awareness have value." That is [[投資沒有標準答案|"only what you realize yourself becomes the Way"]] in different words.

## The four stages of understanding

Elsewhere in the session James laid out four stages (noting "I covered this on YouTube before"):[^1]

| Stage | State |
|---|---|
| 1 | **You don't know that you don't know** (and indeed you don't) |
| 2 | **You think you know** (learned a little, feel invincible) → **the most dangerous stage, where you lose the most money** |
| 3 | **You know that you don't know** |
| 4 | **You don't know that you know** (mastery) |

"Most people stop at stages one and two." Stage two is exactly [[投資的七個層級|the "level 2" of the seven levels]] — believing your technique is superior, with a 99% casualty rate. The only way up is sustained doubt and study.

## Know a little more every day

He closes it on a character breakdown: **the Chinese word 智 (wisdom) is 知 (to know) above 日 (day) — know a little more each day and wisdom accumulates.** "Don't think a little a day is too slow. We have so many days in a life; **the change will be enormous**." And in investing the payoff isn't linear — see [[選錯指數也會變窮|the ladder of understanding]].[^1]

> How James uses AI himself: he demonstrated getting OpenAI to make a dental appointment (it even reports the price) and to book flights and hotels — "at the end it just asks me to confirm the order." He says it's precisely because he is "both an investor and an industry person" that he can see this shift in business models — **which is why he opened the room to warn people that day**.[^1]

## 2026: don't bring me AI's answers — but do tell AI to answer with CLEC's philosophy

A year later, AI is part of members' daily lives, and James's stance comes down to two lines that look opposite but agree:

**One: don't send me AI's answers to decode (00581).** Many people ask an AI, paste its long reply into an email and ask "Teacher, is this right?" — he never answers those: "I don't know how you asked it or why it answered that way." It's the same as not bringing him a banker's, an investment newsletter's or an influencer's advice. **Either question the AI until you understand, or don't follow it**; if you can't think it through yourself, the advice is useless to you. The deck is harsher: "When an ignorant person who can't ask good questions asks AI, AI gives an idiotic answer; you bring me the idiotic answer — do I need to reply?" — **I think, therefore I am; only by thinking for yourself do you exist.**[^2]

**Two: but you can tell AI to answer with CLEC's philosophy (00582).** CLEC's videos and texts are now spread across the internet worldwide; "every AI should have learned our investing philosophy." So the deck suggests opening your question with "**Please answer according to CLEC's investment philosophy regarding…**"; to find a video, ask it to "search every platform for CLEC video number xxxxx and give the link." "You can do all that — no need to ask me."[^3]

Both lines say the same thing: **AI is a tool, not your brain.** In 00580: "Even with AI, you can't believe it completely; you still need to think — **you manage the AI, not let the AI manage you**." Don't be a parrot; 99.999% of people believe whatever they hear.[^4]

### Will AI leave our children without jobs? (00582)

Someone worried what children should learn when AI is so capable. James says not to panic: calculators were once banned from exams, and when Excel arrived people said accountants would be jobless — "I tell you, accountants got busier, because there's more they can do." When he graduated, Taiwan offered mostly PC or semiconductor jobs; now there's far more to do — **"Every generation has its opportunities, and they keep growing. I'm actually a bit jealous of the new generation."**[^3]

He also notes the other side: **"AI can do everything, but people allow"** — some things humans won't hand to AI, not because it can't. He'd rather reply to emails himself, typos and all, "because that shows it's me"; perhaps one day laws will bar AI from raising children or providing end-of-life care — "When you draw your last breath, would you want an AI at your side?"[^3]
