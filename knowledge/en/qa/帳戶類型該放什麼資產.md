> **In 30 seconds**: Put the **most aggressive, fastest-growing** assets (QLD, QQQ) in the **tax-free Roth IRA**, and the **conservative** ones (BOXX, [[QQQI]], cash) in the **Traditional IRA or a taxable account**. That way the most tax-advantaged room goes to the assets that grow the most. The pairing trick: **one dollar of QLD in Roth against one dollar of BOXX (cash) in the Traditional account keeps overall [[Beta]] at 1.0** — account location decides "which asset enjoys the tax break," without changing overall risk.[^1]

## Situational variants (how people actually ask it)

- "My Roth IRA holds BOXX and QQQI — is that paired right?"
- "I'm doing a Roth conversion of $200k — which asset should I move in, and what do I buy after?"
- "Should a high-dividend fund like QQQI go in the Roth or the Traditional account?"

## Principle: aggressive into Roth, conservative in Traditional/taxable

Growth in a Roth is **tax-free for life**, so stuff the "will grow the most" assets in there to let the biggest gains come out untaxed; the Traditional IRA is taxed on withdrawal, so it suits conservative, low-growth positions.[^1]

- **Roth**: QLD (2× QQQ), QQQ — most aggressive. "You'd rather hold all BOXX in the Traditional account and all QLD in the Roth."
- **Traditional IRA / taxable**: BOXX, [[QQQI]], cash — conservative, cash-flow-producing, or safety-cushion positions.
- **Neither QQQI nor BOXX should go in the Roth**; the most conservative thing in a Roth should still be QQQ.

## Use "pairing" to keep overall Beta

Don't look at each account's allocation in isolation — look at the **whole**. Pair the two sides: **one dollar of QLD (Beta 2.0) in Roth against one dollar of BOXX (Beta 0) in the Traditional account → combined Beta 1.0**, the risk of one dollar of QQQ, but with all the growth in the tax-free Roth.[^1]

Look at how much is in the Roth first to size the QLD:[^1]

- E.g. Roth has $200k → all QLD; Traditional holds a matching $200k of BOXX; put any remainder in QQQ.
- If the Roth is far larger than the Traditional (e.g. Roth $3M, Traditional $1M) → the Roth might become $1M QLD + $1M QQQ + $1M cash, with the Traditional's $1M all cash. **Unless the Roth is larger than the Traditional, the Roth shouldn't hold cash.**
- If your Roth is still small: it should be **all QLD**.

> Note: James's own bespoke setup is — the brokerage account holds almost no cash, fully invested, generating cash flow via [[質押借款|stock pledge]]; the cash sits in the Traditional and Roth accounts. That's his personal approach, not a general rule.[^1]

## Same method, 2024 version used TQQQ (the pairing ratio moves with it)

This "leverage in Roth, cash in Traditional, combined Beta 1.0" method was already taught in 2024 — just with **TQQQ (3×, Beta 3)** instead of QLD (2×) as the leverage tool. **The pairing ratio moves with the leverage multiple; the target net Beta doesn't:**[^2]

- 00492's worked example: total assets $540k, keep 20% cash → **Roth $180k, all in TQQQ**, **Traditional holds $360k cash** (= $180k × 2). Because TQQQ is 3×, it takes twice the cash to neutralize it back to Beta 1.0.
- Compare this page's 00570 QLD version: QLD is 2×, so the pairing is **1:1** (Roth $200k QLD ↔ Traditional $200k BOXX).
- The same evolution as assets grow: "assets reach $2M, Roth has $400k, Traditional has $600k left → Roth holds $200k QLD **or** $100k TQQQ" — **double the leverage multiple, halve the dollar amount.**
- The same session also restates the order: "**if you're pledging, the leveraged fund in Roth should stay in Roth**"; "the converted-to-Roth portion can hold leveraged funds, **Traditional can hold money market** — Traditional won't grow as fast, so 10% instead of 14% is fine."

> In other words: **what to remember is the rule — "aggressive into Roth, conservative in Traditional, combined Beta 1.0" — not a fixed set of dollar amounts.** The tools (TQQQ / QLD / BOXX) and ratios are just that rule's implementation at the time.
