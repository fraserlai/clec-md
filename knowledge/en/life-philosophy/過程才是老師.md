> **In 30 seconds**: Many of life's events don't teach us through their **"goal"** — the beautiful scenery on a trip, the championship, the Oscar statuette, the diploma, even a pile of money in the bank teach you nothing. What teaches you is the **"process."** Likewise, **"getting rich" itself teaches you nothing about life** (having NT$300M or US$50M sitting in the bank teaches you nothing); but **"the process of pursuing wealth"** teaches you the most — what greed and fear are, how to understand risk and risk control when the market is at its hardest, how to reach **"the market has nothing to do with me, stay calm, happy when it rises, comfortable when it falls, at ease."**[^1]

## The goal doesn't teach you; the process does

This is the theme of 00575. James builds up "**the result/goal has no educational value; the process does**" with a string of everyday examples:[^1]

- **Travel**: seeing beautiful landscapes teaches you nothing — you snap a photo, put it in your phone, and that's it. What you actually learn is the "side effect" — getting along with people, understanding others, appreciating a restaurant chef's craftsmanship, and completing the process of planning a trip.
- **Sports**: who wins or loses, which team takes the title, teaches you nothing; **teamwork, sportsmanship, grace in winning and losing** are what the game really teaches.
- **An Oscar**: the trophy teaches the actor nothing; the whole process of an acting career is where the most is learned — **"ten minutes on stage takes ten years of work off stage."**
- **A diploma**: the certificate teaches you nothing; the human relationships you build at school are what you truly learn.

## Applied to investing: getting rich doesn't teach you; the pursuit does

Put the same ruler on investing, and you get CLEC's core investing psychology:[^1]

- **"Getting rich" is a result, with no educational value**: money in the bank won't teach you greed, fear, or risk control. Many people fixate only on the goal ("I want to make a lot of money") yet never learn to control emotion and risk.
- **"The process of pursuing wealth" is the real teacher**: in the process of accumulating wealth and living through market ups and downs, you finally come to understand —
  - what **greed** is, what **fear** is;
  - what it means to **not be ruled by market fear**;
  - when the market is at its hardest, what **risk** and **risk control** are;
  - and the final state: **"the market has nothing to do with me," stay calm, happy when it rises, comfortable when it falls, at ease.**
- And it's a process that gets **steadier over time** — "over the whole process of investing you get better and steadier, and then you're at ease."

This is exactly why CLEC repeatedly stresses that **stable emotion matters more than returns** and that **the only controllables are allocation and emotion** (see [[投資心理學散戶為什麼被割韭菜|Investing psychology]]), and why you use the identity "**I am a long-term investor**" to make holding-to-the-death natural (see [[身份認同與長期投資|Identity and long-term investing]]) — the point was never the money at the finish line, but growing yourself into someone who can hold with peace of mind.

## Member stories: how the process changed lives before anyone was rich

00575 has two member stories that confirm "the process itself is already teaching and changing you" — neither has reached the finish line, yet the process has already changed their state of mind:[^2]

- **Homemaker a homemaker member**: she calls herself slow, often can't follow the lectures ("I understand every word a senior member says, but put together I don't know what he's saying"), yet she listens while washing dishes, mopping, and cooking, and when she can't follow she asks [[世界不是真的|ChatGPT to explain it using CLEC's philosophy]]. She says the biggest change isn't her assets but that she has "**found meaning in life** and no longer despairs about the future" — echoing what James taught this session: "what's precious isn't the goal of being rich, but the process."
- **A corporate executive (PhD returnee)**: he only encountered CLEC in late May, and within two months surrendered the insurance he should cancel, sold a house, and put every deployable dollar into the Nasdaq index ([[全球納斯達克100指數基金對照|513100]]), keeping some cash as a risk buffer ([[韓信點兵借貸順序|Han Xin muster]]). He says the most obvious change is that **his stress at work dropped, his mood lightened, and life feels full of hope** — a transformation that also happened *during the process, before getting rich.*

> ⚠️ This is a synthesis of investing psychology and life attitude, not personalized investment advice; member stories are personal experiences, and surrendering insurance, selling property, or concentrating into a single asset all carry their own risks and preconditions (secure your cash flow first, see [[十五年現金流]]).
